Two ideas that did not survive contact with the data

Both were added to fix the post-2024 breakdown: a macro regime read from traded prices, and O'Neil's own rule that you buy leaders of leading industry groups. Each is scored on the whole run and on both halves. Neither improves both, which is the bar a real edge has to clear.

Walk-forward: what fitting parameters is actually worth

Fit on a trailing 18 months, trade the next six with whatever that window chose, step forward, repeat. Seven folds, none of them scored on data used to choose their own settings — against the alternative of picking one sensible setting and never touching it again.

Method, and what to distrust

Point-in-time discipline

Signals on day t use only bars up to and including day t's close. Discretionary orders — entries and trend-break exits — fill at day t+1's open. The 7% stop and the 25% target are standing orders placed in advance, so they fill intraday against that day's high and low; a gap through the stop fills at the open, not the stop price.

The tradeable universe on any date is the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 membership as recorded in the Wikipedia revision of the constituent list that was live on that date. Names that were later acquired or delisted are still present in the old revisions, so the strategy can and does buy companies that no longer exist.

Which CAN SLIM letters are implemented

L leader — IBD-style relative strength, weighted to the most recent quarter, percentile-ranked against every other name in that day's universe. Buys require RS 80+. N new high — a first close above the resistance of a 5–13 week base, within 15% of the 52-week high. S supply and demand — breakout volume 1.4× the 50-day average. M market direction — a three-state regime read off the S&P 500's own moving averages, which gates new buying entirely.

Exits depart from O'Neil in one place. His 20-25% profit target is replaced by a 15% trailing stop measured from the highest close since entry; the 7% hard stop, the 8-week rule and the 50-day-line break are unchanged. The target capped every winner that mattered. The 15% figure is not tuned — anything from 15% to 25% is the same decision, and the trailing stop's real contribution is only that it stops capping winners.

C, A and I — current and annual earnings growth, and institutional sponsorship — are not implemented. They need point-in-time fundamentals that are not available for companies that have since been delisted, and faking them with today's figures would be exactly the lookahead this backtest exists to avoid. This is a technical reading of O'Neil and should be read as one.

Costs, and the biases that survive

Five basis points of slippage on every fill, both sides; zero commission. No borrowing cost, no market impact — position sizes are small relative to a $20M/day liquidity floor, so impact should be minor, but it is not zero.

Every parameter here comes from O'Neil's published numbers rather than from fitting this sample — 7% stop, 20–25% target, the 8-week rule, RS 80. That limits overfitting but does not eliminate it: the base-detection window and the volume threshold are still choices, and a single 4.7-year run on one universe is a small sample to judge any strategy on.
