There are three boundaries, plus the research objective.

JevA bounded inferenceInterpret supplied evidence against explicit criteria; return a typed label, score, or probability. The proposed runtime is fixed before evaluation: code builds an as-of packet, Jev returns a judgment, and code applies a frozen policy. Claude develops that workflow offline. If Claude writes a fresh interpretation for each historical event, it becomes a second runtime model; that model needs its own frozen prompt, version, data boundary, and attribution.

The fairness test is a substitution: hold the data, trigger, risk policy, and execution constant; replace only the judgment source. Compare Jev with a constant/base-rate response and simple computed features. A frozen Claude runtime comparator is useful only if the research question includes Jev’s cost or quality relative to Claude. It must see the same evidence.

03 / Separate the claims

Correct interpretation, predictive skill, and useful trading are different achievements.

Test A · interpretation

Does it read the evidence correctly?

Examples: a source explicitly withdraws guidance; a chart meets a stated discretionary base rubric.

Judge against blinded human labels, with disagreement adjudicated. Measure class-specific precision/recall, appropriate agreement for ordinal ratings, and errors among accepted versus abstained cases.

A pass earns: a validated classifier for this task and population.

Test B · prediction

Does its answer anticipate an outcome?

Examples: a precisely specified recovery occurs within a fixed horizon; a candidate outperforms a defined comparison.

For probabilities, use Brier score or log loss against a training-only base rate, calibration and discrimination. Compare with simple features on identical events. For ranking, evaluate within the actual decision sets.

A pass earns: evidence of incremental predictive information.

Test C · decision value

Does using it improve the policy?

Compare identical simulations with and without Jev’s contribution, after costs and under the same risk constraints.

Report paired changes in net return, drawdown, turnover, exposure and loss tails. Include a comparable-exposure control where applicable. Keep the pre-agreed split-half check as an additional robustness condition.

A pass earns: evidence the chosen integration helps this strategy.

An attractive chart may be recognized accurately while having no predictive value. A good forecast can still lose money with bad payoffs or poor execution. A profitable full strategy can owe all its profits to the rules around a useless model. These tests locate the source of success or failure.

The current questions mix these levels: “proper base” is rubric recognition; “quality” blends several aesthetic properties with an implied future advance; “follow through over the coming weeks” is an underspecified forecast. Pooled 20/60-day return correlations alone cannot diagnose all three. The evaluation scripts that generated the brief’s correlations are not present in src/, so their exact label and censoring rules are not reproducible from the checked-in runner.

Confidence is not a position size. A Noul is the probability of its stated proposition. Score and Choice confidence describe concentration of the returned distribution; Noul has no separate confidence field. Being confident that a setup is poor is not a reason to buy more. Nor is confidence that a document says “guidance withdrawn” the probability its stock falls. Trading calibration must be measured on trading outcomes. Confidence · Noul · Score.

TypeSafe documents Jev as text-only and explicitly cautions about numeric precision and numeric representations. Sending weekly OHLC values is therefore not a demonstrated substitute for chart vision. A narrow textual interpretation is a more natural fit; whether that interpretation predicts returns remains unproven. Supported state · Known limitations.

04 / Verified responsibilities of the coding agent

The brief overstates what the current rig establishes.

Confirmed lookahead in the baseline. The simulator obtains today’s position limit from today’s closing market regime, then uses that limit to admit yesterday’s orders at today’s open. In a two-day synthetic replay, changing only the later index close changes the earlier buy count from one to zero. The published result needs recomputation after repair; the size and direction of the numerical impact have not been measured.

Evidence: src/backtest.py:89–119, src/strategy.py:137–159. Read-only reproduction: .lavish/audit_jev_boundary.py.

The exit story is not established

The saved ledger contains 43 exits labelled “broke 50dma,” averaging +5.325% per trade and totaling +$45,062.45 realized P&L. This contradicts the brief’s “roughly breakeven” summary. Even if they had averaged zero, they could have prevented much larger losses. Their realized return is not the causal effect of the rule.

A fair exit test compares selling now with a fully specified continuation from that same decision time. A profitable trade can have been exited too late; a losing trade can have been exited correctly. “This rule is demonstrably costing money” should be withdrawn until the counterfactual is measured.

Evidence: data/result.json, all SELL rows whose reason begins with “broke 50dma”.

The evaluation population differs from the portfolio’s population

Both Jev result tables contain 635 rows across 361 tickers and 394 dates. Of those events, 88 are outside that date’s snapshot membership. Only 283 pass membership, the existing buyable flag, and non-RED regime together; an actual portfolio can consider fewer because of holdings and slots.

Broad screening is permissible if the question is explicitly about that broader population. It is not a direct estimate of Jev’s value at the actual buy decision. There are 633 complete forward 20-session closes but only 614 complete forward 60-session closes in the cache. Truncating the last observations into shorter horizons would change the target.

RS ranks are computed over the entire panel of ever-selected names before point-in-time membership gating. Thus knowledge of future membership affects the reference set. The quarterly constituent lists also cannot prove exact daily eligibility.

Evidence: src/jev_judge.py:26–32, src/jev_judge2.py:30–34, src/strategy.py:121–133.

The representation is neither neutral nor a complete chart

The summary begins by asserting a consolidation breakout before asking whether the base is genuine. The input population is already heavily filtered for trend, depth, volume and price. Restriction to that pool limits the question to incremental skill conditional on the existing screen.

The 16-bar weekly representation loses daily sequence and gives no completed-session count for the newest week. Of the 635 events, 498 are not Friday events, so the newest bar is generally partial. Daily volume ratios are averaged; it is not a complete weekly-volume history. The cutoff itself is correctly applied before resampling, so this particular issue is information loss and ambiguity, not future bars leaking through resampling.

The claimed five-week minimum base is not directly enforced as base age: BASE_MIN is used as a recent-cross exclusion window. In the judged set, 275 rows have computed base_len_wk below five. The model should be tested against the actual event definition, not the prose description of it.

Evidence: src/jev.py:95–114, src/jev_judge2.py:39–45, src/strategy.py:85–117.

Versioning does not yet support a defensible model experiment

The cache key hashes question-version, kind and state, but not the question text or model ID. A prompt edit can silently reuse old answers unless the global version is bumped. The client does not pin a model in code; the installed SDK defaults to a moving alias unless configuration overrides it. The cache discards the resolved model version, raw state, question schema, and Score/Choice distributions.

Cache hits repeat the saved output; that is not the same as a fully reproducible experiment. Persist the canonical request, frozen model version, response distributions, preprocessing version, source hashes and timing metadata. Existing sparse cache records cannot prove the full provenance of old responses.

Evidence: src/jev.py:45–52, src/jev.py:124–143. TypeSafe model version guidance.

Additional limits to preserve in any conclusion

The optional chandelier stop uses today’s ATR while simulating a standing intraday order. ATR includes that day’s range, so this branch also needs a previous-close boundary; it is inactive in the shipped percentage-trailing baseline.

The walk-forward harness resets positions at each fold and carries only equity. It measures a sequence of reset portfolios, not uninterrupted adaptive holdings, and needs an explicit liquidation/carry policy. The displayed Sharpe is CAGR divided by volatility, not a conventional mean excess-return Sharpe. Neither issue is a Jev failure.

Forty-nine missing price histories cannot all be explained as verified bankruptcies or delistings from this code. Symbol changes and entity mapping also need investigation. Their bias should not be described as known to be “small.”

Evidence: src/backtest.py:152–157, src/walkforward.py:85–98, src/metrics.py:24–40, cached universe versus panel.

05 / A testable delegation

Two concrete contracts, with different prerequisites.

Preferred model fit · requires new data

Interpret an explicit guidance withdrawal

Trigger: a newly available issuer disclosure relevant to a candidate or held position.

Code supplies: a short original passage; any necessary prior-guidance passage for the same reporting period; neutral entity IDs; independently checked availability and source identifiers. Arithmetic, period alignment and retrieval happen before Jev.

Jev answers: whether the supplied passage explicitly says previously issued guidance no longer applies. If grounding is needed, choose a supporting passage from a supplied finite list, including “no support.” Distinguish a statement in the document from an inference about the business.

Immediate scorecard: agreement with independently adjudicated source labels, false positives, missing-evidence errors, probability calibration and abstention coverage.

Trading scorecard later: whether that feature improves a frozen decision policy relative to the same policy without it. A correctly recognized withdrawal is not automatically a sell: the news may already be priced in.

Present status: no source corpus and no independent labels. This is a proposed acquisition and evaluation task, not an existing capability.

Available price inputs · forecasting unproven

Forecast a precisely defined recovery

Trigger: a held position first qualifies for the existing 50-day discretionary exit, after the timing repair. Restrict to positions surviving that day’s standing stops.

Code supplies: recent daily returns/ranges/volume ratios; precomputed moving-average distances and slopes; depth and persistence of the breach; benchmark and peer-relative behavior; entry/peak/stop distances. A weekly summary is optional and must mark incomplete weeks.

Illustrative target: after decision close t, within the next ten sessions, does the close first recover to the frozen t-day MA50 before the low touches the protective stop frozen at t? If recovery and stop occur on the same daily bar, stop takes precedence. No recovery by the deadline counts as no; unavailable complete horizons are censored.

Immediate scorecard: Brier score, calibration and discrimination against a training-only base rate and a simple regularized numerical model on the same inputs. This is a forecast of that exact event, not an aesthetic rating.

Trading scorecard later: compare next-open exit with one bounded defer policy. Keep active hard/trailing protection, define the deadline and next-open liquidation, log opportunity cost, and test complete portfolios. Recovery probability alone does not supply the payoff distribution.

Present status: raw prices exist, but the event dataset and labels do not. Ten sessions and the barrier definition are examples, not a preregistered or tested choice. Forty-three baseline exit observations are too few for broad tuning.

For the numerical contract, Jev’s value could come from combining familiar patterns differently from a small fitted model. That is a hypothesis, not something guaranteed by the “System One” description. If all the rubric’s predicates can be computed exactly, execute those predicates in code and do not add an unnecessary model step.

Delegation requires an action boundary, too. A classifier first runs in shadow mode. On a missing, invalid, late, or unusable answer, the fixed baseline action applies. An exit experiment could permit only a bounded deferral of the discretionary 50-day exit; it cannot cancel the hard stop or add risk. Keep position sizes fixed while testing that one decision.

The right test for an exit is incremental value from the trigger, not the trade’s gain since its original entry. From an equal initial state, simulate each permitted action under the same assumptions. Then verify the complete portfolio, because retaining a position consumes a slot and capital and changes subsequent opportunities.

Value of Jev = result with Jev judgment − result with the comparison judgment

Both sides use the same historical evidence, mechanical rules, costs, and risk constraints. Similar exposure alone is not proof of skill, but it helps separate selection from merely holding more risk.

06 / Research discipline

Repair the measurement, then earn one delegation.

  1. Repair and freeze the baseline contract. Correct the reproduced timing leak, the universe reference set, model provenance and mismatched descriptions. Recompute the existing configurations once under the repaired simulator. This is measurement repair, not a new parameter search. Keep old outputs identified as pre-repair.
  2. Choose the smallest question with an observable answer. The guidance example is closer to Jev’s documented strengths but needs a corpus. The exit example can use existing prices, but needs a defined event and is not justified by the brief’s exit-return argument. Same-day reranking has only 12 observed shortage days; sizing from confidence is premature.
  3. Build a capability evaluation before attaching money. Develop the prompt and representation on development data. Keep independent annotations separate from model outputs. Compare with deterministic logic where possible and a simple baseline. Use synthetic known cases to check parsing and representation; synthetic success does not establish market forecasting skill.
  4. Freeze all choices before a new evaluation. Record the eligible population, outcome, horizon, censoring, model/version, schema, prompt, threshold, fallback, baselines and primary metric. A semantically reasonable alternate prompt still counts as another experiment.
  5. Use chronology and respect overlapping labels. Purge training examples whose future-outcome window crosses the test boundary; keep related issuer episodes together and quantify uncertainty with time/event blocks. Repeated calls, nearby dates and two framings are not independent observations. Preserve the agreed “both halves” check, but do not treat it as a substitute for independence.
  6. Separate development from confirmation honestly. The brief already reports experiments and structural decisions across 2022–2026. Newly naming a historical slice “held out” cannot erase that exposure. Historical walk-forward can provide exploratory robustness, but a strong confirmation should use an actually unexamined population or a prospective, frozen paper record. A larger universe is another research change and was previously declined.
  7. Promote only a measured component. A classifier may succeed without improving trading. A null can be the correct outcome. Publish the technical rule backtest under its own name and describe any Jev experiment by the exact decision it evaluated.

Removing ticker and date reduces obvious model-recall contamination, but does not prove its absence. Distinctive price sequences, dollar turnover and recognizable documents may identify a company or episode. Identity guessing is a useful diagnostic; inability to guess is not a certificate. The developer also knows famous historical winners. A prospective record is the cleanest control for both sources of hindsight.

Recommendation: treat Jev as a potential interpreter of supplied evidence. The current dataset supports technical forecasting research but does not validate that role. Repair the evaluator first; then test one explicit claim. My earlier preference to immediately move Jev to exits was stronger than the evidence supports.
